A customer opens a chat with your business in WhatsApp and taps the call button. The call arrives at
SignalWire, runs whatever resource you attached to the number, and records, transcribes, and shows up
in your logs the way the rest of your traffic does.

WhatsApp calling uses the same Meta-verified business number as
[WhatsApp messaging](/docs/platform/messaging/whatsapp), so a number your customers already message
can take their calls too. It handles calls that customers place to your business number from
WhatsApp.

## Before you start

You need a [WhatsApp business number connected to your Space](/docs/platform/messaging/whatsapp/onboarding),
voice [enabled on that number](/docs/platform/voice/whatsapp/enable-voice), and a
[resource](/docs/platform/resources) to answer the call.

## How a call reaches your application

Attaching a resource to a WhatsApp number as its calling handler gives the number an address. When a
call comes in, SignalWire runs that resource, passing the caller's number and your business number as
the call's `from` and `to`.

Your business number arrives with a `whatsapp:` prefix and the caller's number arrives as plain
E.164, so a resource that serves several numbers can tell which channel a call came in on:

```yaml
- cond:
    - when: '%{call.to} =~ /^whatsapp:/'
      then:
        - play:
            url: "say:Thanks for calling us on WhatsApp."
```

From there the call behaves like any other. The same SWML and cXML verbs apply, recording and
transcription work, and [AI Agents](/docs/platform/ai) answer WhatsApp calls the way they answer
calls to a phone number.

## What to know before you build

WhatsApp calls are **audio only** — a caller cannot start a video call to your business number, even
when the resource answering is a video room.

They also do not support **hold**. Putting a WhatsApp call on hold does not behave the way it does on
a phone call, so leave hold and hold music out of flows that handle them.
