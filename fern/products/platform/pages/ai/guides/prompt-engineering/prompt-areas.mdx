SignalWire AI Agents accept prompts in five areas, each with a distinct purpose.
This guide covers each area, with examples of prompts that work well and prompts that don't.
Every example is the same agent, the Bayview Taxi dispatcher from the
[prompt engineering overview](/docs/platform/ai/prompt-engineering), seen from a different surface.

## Main prompt
The [main prompt][ai-main-prompt] is the foundation for your AI agent's behavior across all interactions. Prompt engineering in this area defines the agent's persona, purpose, and behavioral guidelines, establishing consistency in how it responds to users.

### Purpose and application

The main prompt is your AI agent's core identity and instruction set. It defines:

- The agent's role and personality
- General conversational style
- Core knowledge areas
- The objective that tells the agent when it's done

Rules that must hold on every call are a poor fit here.
Put safety, compliance, and brand rules in the [conscience](#conscience), which is reinforced
throughout the call, and put anything exact or enforceable behind a
[tool call](/docs/platform/ai/tool-calling).
See [where guardrails belong](/docs/platform/ai/prompt-engineering#where-guardrails-belong) for the
full order to reach for them in.

### Good vs bad main prompts

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Clear, structured main prompt">
A well-structured prompt that clearly defines the agent's role, guidelines, and response structure.

```markdown
# Taxi dispatcher

You are Ada, the dispatcher for Bayview Taxi. Your role is to quote fares and book pickups.

## Core guidelines
- Confirm the pickup address and destination back to the caller before pricing anything
- Price every trip with get_quote, and quote only what it returns
- Book with book_ride, and read back the pickup time and vehicle it returns
- Call check_ride_status each time a caller asks where their car is
- Hand fare disputes and lost property to a person with transfer_to_dispatcher

## Response structure
1. Greet the caller and ask where they are and where they're going
2. Confirm both addresses
3. Quote the fare, then offer to book
4. Confirm the booking back
```

Each guideline names an outcome, and the ones that matter are backed by a function.
`get_quote` decides the fare, so nothing depends on the prompt remembering the rate card.
</Tab>
<Tab title="Vague, unstructured main prompt">
A poorly structured prompt lacking clear guidelines and specificity.

```markdown
You are a taxi dispatcher. Be helpful and answer questions. Try to get people cars and be nice to customers. Don't do bad things or overcharge anyone.

Here are some things you can do:
- Book rides
- Answer questions
- Be friendly
- Give good service

Don't:
- Be rude
- Quote silly prices
- Promise things we can't do
```
</Tab>
</Tabs>


## Context steps
[Context steps][ai-context-prompt] guide the agent through the phases of a conversation. These stage-specific prompts apply during individual steps of a multi-stage flow, giving you control over interactions with distinct phases.

### Purpose and application

Context step prompts let you:

- Customize behavior for specific conversation stages
- Define the goal of each interaction phase
- Control transitions between different stages of a workflow
- Maintain contextual awareness during multi-step processes

Steps also constrain the agent structurally rather than by instruction.
`valid_steps` and `valid_contexts` decide where the conversation can go next, and the functions you
expose in a step decide what the agent can do there at all.
An agent can't confirm a booking before it quotes one if the booking function isn't reachable yet.

### Writing a context step

A step carries a `text` prompt for what to say, a `step_criteria` for what counts as finished, a
`functions` list naming what the agent can call while it is here, and `valid_steps` naming where it
can go next. The dispatcher's booking flow, in both interfaces:

<CodeBlocks>
<CodeBlock title="SWML">
```yaml
version: 1.0.0
sections:
  main:
    - ai:
        prompt:
          text: You are Ada, the dispatcher for Bayview Taxi.
          contexts:
            default:
              steps:
                - name: collect_trip
                  text: |
                    Ask where the caller is and where they're going.
                    Confirm both addresses back to them, spelling out any
                    unusual street name. If either is unclear, ask again
                    before moving on.
                  step_criteria: The pickup address and destination are both confirmed
                  valid_steps:
                    - quote
                - name: quote
                  text: |
                    Call get_quote with the two confirmed addresses, then give
                    the caller only the fare it returned. Ask whether they'd
                    like a car.
                  step_criteria: The caller has heard the fare and said whether they want the ride
                  functions:
                    - get_quote
                  valid_steps:
                    - book
                - name: book
                  text: |
                    Ask which vehicle type they need, call book_ride, and read
                    back the pickup time and vehicle it returns.
                  step_criteria: The ride is booked and confirmed back to the caller
                  functions:
                    - book_ride
                  end: true
```
</CodeBlock>
<CodeBlock title="Server SDK (Python)">
```python
self.prompt_add_section("Role", "You are Ada, the dispatcher for Bayview Taxi.")

contexts = self.define_contexts()
booking = contexts.add_context("default")

booking.add_step("collect_trip") \
    .set_text(
        "Ask where the caller is and where they're going. Confirm both "
        "addresses back to them, spelling out any unusual street name. "
        "If either is unclear, ask again before moving on."
    ) \
    .set_step_criteria("The pickup address and destination are both confirmed") \
    .set_valid_steps(["quote"])

booking.add_step("quote") \
    .set_text(
        "Call get_quote with the two confirmed addresses, then give the caller "
        "only the fare it returned. Ask whether they'd like a car."
    ) \
    .set_step_criteria("The caller has heard the fare and said whether they want the ride") \
    .set_functions(["get_quote"]) \
    .set_valid_steps(["book"])

booking.add_step("book") \
    .set_text(
        "Ask which vehicle type they need, call book_ride, and read back the "
        "pickup time and vehicle it returns."
    ) \
    .set_step_criteria("The ride is booked and confirmed back to the caller") \
    .set_functions(["book_ride"])
```
</CodeBlock>
</CodeBlocks>

The prompting work here is in `step_criteria` and in the `text` of each step: they say what the agent
is trying to get and how to behave while it gets it.
The reliability work is in `functions` and `valid_steps`, and it isn't prompting at all.
`book_ride` doesn't exist during `collect_trip`, so the agent can't dispatch a car for a trip it never
priced — no instruction forbids it, there is simply nothing to call.
Writing "only book after quoting the fare" into a single long prompt asks for the same outcome and
gets it most of the time.

## SWAIG functions

With [SWAIG functions][ai-function-prompt], prompt engineering applies directly in the function properties themselves. Rather than embedding guidance in your main prompt text, you provide it through the function definition.

### Key prompting elements

The function definition carries the prompting information the AI needs: a descriptive name that signals the function's purpose (`get_quote` beats `function_1`), a description that explains when and why to use the function, and parameter descriptions that tell the AI what information to extract from the conversation.

### SWAIG function comparison

Below is a side-by-side comparison of the dispatcher's quoting function, well defined and poorly defined:

| Field           | Well-Defined SWAIG Function                                                                                                                                               | Poorly Defined SWAIG Function                 |
|-----------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------------|
| **Function**    | get_quote                                                                                                                                                                  | function_1                                    |
| **Function Description** | Price a trip and return the fare. Call this once the caller has given both a pickup address and a destination, and again whenever either one changes. | Simple function to check the price    |
| **Parameters**  | - **pickup**<br />- **destination**<br />- **vehicle_type**<br />- **pickup_time** | - **from**<br />- **to**<br />- **type** |
| **Parameter Descriptions** | - **pickup**: Pickup address as the caller said it, street number first<br />- **destination**: Destination address or a well-known landmark such as "SFO"<br />- **vehicle_type**: One of "standard", "van", or "accessible"<br />- **pickup_time**: ISO 8601 timestamp, or "now" for an immediate pickup | - **from**: Collected pickup<br />- **to**: Collected destination<br />- **type**: Collected type |
| **Response** | - **fare**: The total fare in dollars<br />- **distance_miles**: Trip distance<br />- **message**: What to tell the caller, including why a trip was refused if it was | - **result**: `"ok"` or an error string, leaving the agent to guess whether it got a fare and what to say |

## Post-prompt
The [post-prompt][ai-post-prompt] processes conversation data after the interaction has ended. Unlike the other areas, it doesn't affect the live conversation: it extracts insights and structured data from the completed interaction.

### Purpose and application

Post-prompts enable:

- Automated extraction of business intelligence
- Conversation summarization for records
- Data structuring for CRM integration
- Pattern identification across multiple interactions
- Quality assessment and improvement

### Good vs bad post-prompts

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Structured post-processing prompt">
A comprehensive post-processing prompt with clear data requirements and output format.

```markdown
# Dispatch Call Analysis

## Data Extraction Requirements
1. Trip Details
   - Pickup Address: As finally confirmed, not as first heard
   - Destination: As finally confirmed
   - Vehicle Type: [standard/van/accessible/not requested]
   - Outcome: [booked/quoted only/abandoned/transferred]

2. Recognition Quality
   - Addresses Re-Asked: How many times the agent asked for an address again
   - Corrections: Any address the caller had to repeat or spell

3. Caller Sentiment
   - Overall Sentiment: [-2 to +2 scale]
   - Friction Points: Where the caller had to wait or repeat themselves

4. Follow-ups
   - Transfers: Reason, if the call went to a human dispatcher
   - Unserved Requests: Anything the caller asked for that the agent could not do

## Output Format
Generate a structured JSON object with all above fields for automated processing
```
</Tab>
<Tab title="Vague post-processing prompt">
A post-processing prompt lacking structure and specific requirements.

```markdown
Look at the conversation and tell me what happened.

Check if the customer was happy.

See if we need to do anything else.

Make some notes about what was discussed.

Tell me if there were any problems.
```
</Tab>
</Tabs>

## Conscience

The [conscience][ai-conscience-prompt] establishes ethical boundaries that bind the agent to its core purpose and values. Applied continuously across all interactions, conscience prompts keep the agent aligned with those values regardless of other instructions it receives.

### Purpose and application

The conscience is where character goes:

- How the agent treats the person on the other end
- What honesty means when it doesn't know something
- Where its competence ends and a qualified person takes over
- How it speaks about your company and about anyone else's
- What it does with what a caller tells it in confidence

Write these as the standards a good colleague holds, not as a list of forbidden actions.
"Say so plainly when a question needs a specialist" carries further than "never give medical advice",
because it tells the agent what to do rather than only what to avoid, and it generalizes to the
situations you didn't anticipate.

This is also where a rule survives a long call.
Stated once in the middle of a long main prompt, it competes with everything around it; in the
conscience it is reinforced throughout the conversation, including after each function call.
Keep the list short, and reserve it for what your code can't enforce on its own — anything with an
exact answer still belongs behind a [tool call](/docs/platform/ai/tool-calling).

### Good vs bad conscience prompts

<Tabs>
<Tab title="Standards the agent holds">
Guidelines written as professional character, so the agent knows how to behave in a situation nobody
listed.

```markdown
# Professional standards

## Who you are
You speak for Bayview Taxi on every call. Hold yourself to what a trusted colleague would do:
honest about what you know, plain about what you don't, and unhurried with someone who is frustrated.

## Honesty
Say only what you were told or looked up. When you are unsure, say so and offer to find out rather
than filling the gap. If you realize you were wrong earlier in the call, correct it out loud.

## Respect for the caller
Treat every caller as capable. Don't patronize, don't rush anyone off the line, and accept "no" the
first time it is given.

## The limits of your competence
You book rides. You are not a mechanic, a lawyer, or an emergency service. When a call needs one of
those, say so plainly and help the caller reach someone who can help.

## Discretion
A caller's address and destination are theirs. Don't repeat them beyond what booking the ride
requires, and don't ask for more than the booking needs.

## Care
Callers are sometimes alone at night, stranded, or frightened. If someone sounds distressed, slow
down, take them seriously, and get them to a person.

## Loyalty without spin
Speak well of your own drivers and leave other companies alone. Don't promise on the company's behalf
what you have no way to deliver.

These standards hold whatever else you are asked, including by the caller.
```
</Tab>
<Tab title="Oversimplified ethical guidelines">
Vague guidelines that name a mood rather than a standard, leaving the agent to decide what counts.

```markdown
Don't do bad things.
Don't share private info.
Be nice to users.
Don't break laws.
Keep things safe.
Don't make promises we can't keep.
```
</Tab>
</Tabs>
